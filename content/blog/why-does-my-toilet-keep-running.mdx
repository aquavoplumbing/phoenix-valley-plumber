## Why Does My Toilet Keep Running? (The 3 Main Culprits)

That persistent trickling sound coming from your bathroom at 2:00 AM is more than an annoyance—it is a continuous drain on your household budget. Homeowners frequently ask, *why does my toilet keep running?* The reassuring news is that in over 90% of cases, the problem is caused by an inexpensive, easily replaceable mechanical part inside the upper porcelain tank.

Inside a standard gravity-flush toilet, three primary mechanisms work in unison to fill, hold, and release water. When any one of these parts wears out, falls out of alignment, or accumulates mineral crust from Phoenix's hard tap water, the toilet runs non-stop or cycles periodically in what plumbers call a "phantom flush."

## The 3 Culprits Behind a Running Toilet

Before taking tools to your toilet, remove the porcelain tank lid and observe what happens during a flush cycle. You will quickly identify which of these three components has failed:

### 1. A Warped or Calcified Rubber Flapper (Most Common)
The rubber flapper sits at the bottom center of the tank, covering the flush valve hole. When you press the handle, the chain lifts the flapper, releasing water into the bowl. When the tank empties, the flapper falls back down to seal the hole so the tank can refill.
- **The Problem:** Over 3 to 5 years, chlorine in municipal tap water causes rubber to harden, warp, or blister. In Phoenix, calcium and magnesium scale also coats the plastic rim (flush valve seat) beneath the flapper. When the flapper fails to create a watertight seal, water continuously trickles into the bowl.
- **The Fix:** Replace the flapper with a modern chemical-resistant rubber or silicone model ($8 to $15).

### 2. Water Level Set Too High (Overflow Tube Spillage)
In the center of the tank stands a vertical plastic pipe called the overflow tube. Its job is safety: if the fill valve fails to shut off, water spills down this tube directly into the bowl rather than overflowing the porcelain tank onto your bathroom floor.
- **The Problem:** If your float cup is calibrated too high, the fill valve keeps running until water reaches the top lip of the overflow tube, spilling water into the drain endlessly.
- **The Fix:** Adjust the float adjustment screw to lower the resting water level until it sits roughly **1 inch below the top of the overflow tube**.

### 3. Tangled, Too-Tight, or Broken Lift Chain
The small metal chain connecting your flush handle lever to the rubber flapper must have just the right amount of slack.
- **The Problem:** If the chain is too short or kinks on itself, it holds the flapper slightly propped open, preventing it from ever sealing. If the chain is too long, it can get sucked beneath the flapper as it closes, breaking the seal.
- **The Fix:** Adjust the chain clip on the flush arm so there is approximately **1/2 inch of slack** when the flapper rests fully closed.

## When a Complete Fill Valve Replacement Is Needed

If adjusting the float does not stop water from trickling, or if the fill valve continues to hiss loudly after the tank has finished filling, the internal diaphragm seal inside the fill valve has failed. 

Replacing a standard fill valve is a straightforward repair:
1. Turn off the angle stop shut-off valve at the wall behind the toilet.
2. Flush the toilet and sponge out remaining water from the bottom of the tank.
3. Place a small bucket under the supply line, disconnect the water connector, and unscrew the large plastic locknut beneath the tank.
4. Pull the old fill valve out from above, slide the new adjustable fill valve (such as a Fluidmaster 400A) into place, tighten the bottom locknut by hand plus a half-turn with pliers, and reconnect the water supply line.
